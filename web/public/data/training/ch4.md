# Training Manual: Chapter 4 - The Competency Chain

## 1. Deeper OD Understanding of the Content

Chapter 4 introduces the concept of the "Competency Chain," emphasizing that organizational problems are rarely isolated. Instead, they are interconnected links in a chain where a compromise in one area (e.g., recruitment) inevitably cascades downstream, creating dysfunction in others (e.g., training, sales, delivery, and brand). In OD terms, this is a systems thinking approach. Organizations often treat symptoms (like poor delivery or low sales margins) without addressing the root cause (such as hiring for operational seat-filling rather than strategic capability). 

The OD insight here is that "BUSYness" is a structural byproduct of these early compromises. When standards are lowered at the entry points of capability-building, the resulting friction creates relentless noise and firefighting. Managers become trapped in operational survival mode rather than functioning as the CEO's cultural representatives. True high performance requires relentless protection of the integrity of every single link in this chain.

## 2. 5 Typical Customer Questions/Dilemmas

1. **"We have an urgent project and need bodies right now. We can't afford to wait for the 'perfect' candidate. Shouldn't we just hire someone to get the work done?"**
2. **"Our top salesperson is leaving unless we promote them to Sales Manager. They know the product better than anyone. Why shouldn't they lead the team?"**
3. **"We're hitting our revenue targets by taking on every client that comes our way, but our margins are shrinking and the team is exhausted. How do we fix profitability?"**
4. **"Our delivery team is constantly making mistakes and missing deadlines. Do we just need a new head of operations to enforce stricter rules?"**
5. **"We want to build a long-term strategic plan, but we're too busy putting out daily fires just to survive. How do we break this cycle?"**

## 3. How a Supportive OD Expert (Manoj Onkar) Should Respond

**Response to Dilemma 1 (Hiring for urgency):**
"I hear the intense pressure you're under to deliver right now. But let's look at what happens when we lower the bar just to fill a seat. When we hire for availability rather than capability, we aren't solving the problem; we're just shifting the burden to the rest of the team who now has to manage, train, and fix the mistakes of a poor fit. That one 'quick fix' fractures your entire Competency Chain. What if, instead, we hold the standard and figure out a temporary way to handle the workload without permanently diluting your organization's DNA?"

**Response to Dilemma 2 (Promoting based on domain excellence):**
"It's completely natural to want to reward your best performer. But domain excellence and leadership capability are two very different games. By promoting them without preparing them to lead, you risk losing your best producer while simultaneously frustrating the team they are meant to guide. Management is about coaching and enforcing standards, not just technical mastery. Let's explore how we can deeply prepare them for leadership, or find alternative ways to recognize their contribution that don't compromise the team's performance."

**Response to Dilemma 3 (Revenue vs. Profitability):**
"Celebrating revenue growth is important, but not all revenue is true business—much of it is just margin-destroying BUSYness. When we chase ill-fitting, complex orders just for the top line, we consume massive operational energy and executive focus for very little return. Let's do a ruthless audit of those contracts. Are they genuinely building your future, or are they creating operational noise that distracts you from your best, most profitable clients?"

**Response to Dilemma 4 (Delivery failures):**
"It’s tempting to view delivery issues as an isolated operations problem that can be fixed with a heavier hand. But in the Competency Chain, delivery failures are almost always the violent manifestation of upstream compromises—like rushed hiring, inadequate training, or selling to the wrong clients. Let's not just put a bandage on the symptoms. We need to trace this back and brutally honestly ask: where did the compromise actually begin?"

**Response to Dilemma 5 (Stuck in daily firefighting):**
"I completely understand that survival feels like the only priority right now. But this cycle of firefighting is exactly what happens when we mortgage tomorrow to survive today. Every time we compromise on a critical link—whether it's onboarding, standards, or strategy—we guarantee more chaos tomorrow. Breaking this cycle requires the courage to pause, reflect, and assert non-negotiable standards today, even when it feels like we don't have the time. Your future depends on the capabilities you protect right now."
